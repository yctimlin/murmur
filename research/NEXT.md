## Test first: task admission before protocol optimization

The highest-priority uncertainty is the experimental task model. All six plain-exec calibrations across four tasks scored 1.0 and took 8.20–19.81 active minutes. C1's frozen duration forecasts were 35/40/65 minutes; the redesigned C2 task forecast was 45 minutes but measured 16.5267 ± 2.9010 (n=3). Neither a longer specification nor a locally validated hidden-test set established real difficulty. The task-size/quality forecast has failed in the observed screening domain; it cannot be transferred confidently to large tasks.

Round 4 should first freeze a small pool of realistic existing-code maintenance tasks with interdependent behavior and reproducible deterministic grading, then preregister plain-exec calibration with at least three repetitions per proposed task. Preserve the original request's 45–90 solo-minute target; separately state the actual admission rule before running (round 3 required score ≤.85 and ≥30 active minutes). Inspect whether the grader leaves measurable headroom and whether the run is uncensored. Keep every failed calibration in the record and charge it to the budget. Do not select the best repeat or continue silently redesigning until one favorable result appears. Local reference/mutant controls establish selected test behaviors, not agent duration or coverage completeness.

## Design and budget consequences

A complete minimal four-arm protocol comparison cannot run all four arms in one block because the runner supports three concurrent jobs. Pre-register connected blocks with a repeated reference arm rather than treating earlier calibration as a paired exec control. For example, alternate [exec, solo, protocol-one] and [protocol-one, protocol-two, protocol-three] blocks on the same task, repeated at least three times. That requires **18 treatment jobs**, plus separate calibration and any additional A/A or bug-fix blocks. It is an illustrative design, not a submitted batch or a claim that three repetitions will be adequate. A bug-fix comparison needs original/base3 pairing; it cannot reuse unmatched baselines as independent controls.

Budget against the largest relevant paired-variance scenario and actual admitted-task cost; the round-3 small-effect priors (+4.5% protocol time, +3.8% third-peer time) may require far more than a 36-job round. If the budget cannot resolve the smallest effect worth acting on, change the research question prospectively, report the limitation, or stop. Do not replace a failed confirmatory endpoint with an exploratory mutation or judge score after seeing outcomes.

Retain every run's wall/active time, input, uncached, output, delivery, exit/cap status, rate limits, leaks and hidden score. Analyze paired blocks, retain scheduled-run results, and report complete clean-block sensitivity without outcome-based removal. Hidden tests and multiple judges are not independent task repetitions. Keep build/test evidence distinct from evaluated quality, and document any harness missingness without silently changing denominators.

The next deliverable is an admitted task and a budgeted, preregistered identification experiment. It is not a mechanism inferred from the current null result, and it does not involve guessing the sealed evaluation tasks or judging rubric.
