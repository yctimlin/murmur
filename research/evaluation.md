# Evaluation and coordination evidence

Research checked October 16, 2026. This is a bounded primary-source survey, not a claim to exhaust every project or paper. Numbers below describe the cited experiment at its publication date, not current product rankings. Interpretations and proposed changes are explicitly separated from observations.

## Studies most relevant to murmur

| Primary source | What it establishes | Limits and implication for murmur |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic, [Patterns and problems in emerging multiagent systems](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems), August 13, 2026 | Studies equal-prompt agents with separate machines, a shared forum and repository. In vulnerability discovery, a coordinating swarm finds more vulnerabilities overall, but comparable yield per token within the same core directories. In game development, prompt variants specifying roles or a CEO made little difference; integration and product quality remained problems. It also observes correlated decisions among similarly configured agents. | Closest topology match found. Different models/tasks and much larger teams prevent direct transfer of numerical effects. More output, more merged changes, and more agent activity are insufficient evidence of better final software. Independent drafts have a plausible diversity rationale, not a proven optimum. |
| Cemri et al., [Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3), version 3 | The taxonomy separates system-design problems, inter-agent misalignment, and verification/termination failures. Its listed failures include incomplete verification, incorrect verification and premature termination. It analyzes traces from several multi-agent frameworks and examines targeted interventions. | A diagnostic taxonomy, not a murmur trial. A deterministic record of what was actually delivered addresses observability of termination; it cannot prove task success. Avoid treating “every peer stopped” as a quality measurement. |
| Kim et al., [Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v1), version 1 | Compares single, independent, centralized, decentralized and hybrid systems under standardized tools and token budgets across Finance-Agent, BrowseComp-Plus, PlanCraft and Workbench. Architecture benefits depend on domain; PlanCraft's sequential planning tasks degrade under all evaluated multi-agent architectures. | These are not repository-wide coding trials. The evidence supports measuring topology against task structure and budget, not a universal agent-count rule. A smaller default is an engineering prior requiring subsequent evaluation. |
| Anthropic, [How we built our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system), June 13, 2025 | An orchestrator delegates parallel independent research using separate contexts and summarized findings. Its internal research evaluation reports a 90.2% improvement over a single-agent comparison. The article reports approximately 15 times chat tokens for multi-agent systems and 4 times chat tokens for agents. It explicitly cautions that coding often offers fewer independent tasks than broad research. | The denominator is chat, **not** solo coding: “15 times solo” would be false. This is internal vendor research with a different architecture, workload and model mix. It cannot establish murmur speedup or cost. |
| Anthropic, [Building a C compiler with a team of parallel Claudes](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler), February 5, 2026 | Reports 16 agents, nearly 2,000 sessions, about two weeks and just under $20,000 in API costs. The engineering account uses independent agent environments, shared git, task locks and extensive executable tests. It reports a substantial compiler while documenting missing functionality, weak optimization and regressions. | A feasibility case study with substantial human harness work, not a controlled comparison with a solo agent. Borrow executable checks and clear integrated artifacts; do not cite it as proof that murmur meets a 1.5–2 times token budget. |
| Xia et al., [Agentless: Demystifying LLM-based Software Engineering Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01489) | The paper presents a localization, repair and validation pipeline and reports strong performance with low inference cost relative to contemporary agent baselines. It also identifies benchmark instances with misleading descriptions or exposed solutions. | Supports a simple baseline and explicit validation rather than assuming additional orchestration is beneficial. Historical benchmark scores are not present-day product comparisons. |

## Evaluation pitfalls and additional sources

- [Quantifying infrastructure noise in agentic coding evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise) (Anthropic, February 5, 2026) holds model, harness and tasks constant while varying resource limits. The reported Terminal-Bench improvement from strict limits to uncapped resources is six percentage points; the article also reports a smaller RAM effect on SWE-bench. **Inference:** preserve resource limits, concurrency, dependency state and time caps when comparing murmur arms. Wall-clock pauses and infrastructure failures must not become hidden model-quality differences.
- [SWE-Bench Pro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941) explicitly targets longer, multi-file enterprise tasks and divides its benchmark into public, held-out and commercial sets. Its reported results belong to that paper's evaluation date. [SWE-Bench Pro Verified](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08149) subsequently reports leakage/reward-hacking and task-quality problems and proposes corrected evaluation. **Inference:** “harder benchmark” does not remove the need to inspect tests, leakage and task validity; retain held-out behavioral checks and artifact review.
- [OpenAI's Multi-agent API guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/responses-multi-agent) describes focused independent contexts, warns that extra agents can increase token usage, and identifies shared mutable state and sequential reasoning as poor fits. These are current API product docs, **not** authoritative evidence for every detail of installed Codex CLI 0.162.1. The dedicated Codex source audit establishes that distinction.
- Murmur's [validation results](evidence/val-RESULTS.md) discard both lean variants under their predefined keep rule; its earlier [lean2 results](evidence/lean2-RESULTS.md) look favorable, while [the experiment log](evidence/LOG.md) records the subsequent reversal and warns about pauses and caching. **Inference:** select improvements for narrow demonstrated benefits, and label end-to-end token or quality gains unverified until replicated on fresh tasks.

## Ranked proposals from this evidence

1. **Measure exact delivered state and total run usage.** Record the final shared commit, per-peer integration state, execution outcome, input/cached/output usage where observable, and explicit unknown fields. This follows directly from verification/termination failure categories and the local keep-rule reversal. It is measurement infrastructure; it does not itself establish a speedup.
2. **Reduce duplicated execution through specific mechanisms that remain correct.** Start peers without irrelevant launcher conversation, preserve selected model settings, and attach check evidence to the actual tested revision. Research gives the rationale; installed source and deterministic tests must establish behavior. Never reuse a test result solely because a human-readable command matches.
3. **Use fewer peers as a conservative default with an explicit override.** Local experiments support lower token pressure in some tasks, but their final validation does not prove equal quality or the target token ratio. Benchmark this separately from context and tooling changes.
4. **Keep large-work integration review executable and artifact based.** Compare final shared revision against tested revision; retain dirty/unpublished peer state as diagnostic data. Existence of PR.md or a clean exit is not a correctness gate. A repository-level check cannot validate subjective requirements or undelivered untracked files.
5. **Do not add another layer of prescriptive coordination prompts as the primary optimization.** Both the closest external swarm study and local failed prompt interventions limit confidence in that route. Task-specific role templates, universal auto-routing and forced model choices would exceed the current evidence.

## Reproducible local draft experiment

The a6 draft is [result.py](../src/murmur/result.py), with [real-git fixture tests](../tests/test_result.py). Its initial focused command was `.venv/bin/pytest -q tests/test_result.py tests/test_run.py`: 14 passed in 4.69 seconds (tool output recorded during this run). These checks invoke the fake Codex executable and local git repositories; they make zero model/API calls. They test unchanged output, pushed work, local unpushed work, dirty tracked files, stale clone heads, missing repositories and recording after Codex failure. Test runtime is wall time, not a model performance experiment. Development-agent token cost is not available from this peer's tools and is **unverified**. No claim of downstream task-quality or token improvement is made.
